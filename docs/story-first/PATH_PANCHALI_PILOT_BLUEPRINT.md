# ORIGIN Story-First Pilot — Pather Panchali

**Working chapter title:** The Film That Almost Didn't Exist  
**Historical subject:** Satyajit Ray's debut feature, from idea to reception, c. 1950–1956  
**State:** Research-backed editorial design / validation pilot; **NOT V7**  
**Production safety:** The deployed V6 website and APK must remain unchanged. Work is isolated in this Git branch.

## Why we're changing direction

The V6 app provides a vivid set of filmmaking experiments and historic-looking environments, but its six short life chapters do not yet make a viewer understand Ray's lived creative journey. The pilot reverses the priorities: **documented human journey → meaningful production problem → relevant interactive lesson.**

## Eight connected scenes

| Beat | Time | Narrative question | Interactive investigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. A film on paper | 1950 | Can a novel become a film made with non-star performers on real locations? | Book, visual treatment, and the lesson Ray drew from *Bicycle Thieves* |
| 2. No producer | 1950–52 | How do you move forward when pitching fails? | Compare more pitching with producing evidence; reveal Ray's described choice |
| 3. The first shoot | Oct 1952 | How does a largely inexperienced film crew make its first image? | Meet the roles involved in the train-discovery scene |
| 4. The vanishing field | 1952–54 | What if the real location changes overnight? | Original illustrative continuity slider, followed by sourced outcome |
| 5. The empty reel | 1953 | What if money stops before the story ends? | Explore personal borrowing, pawning, halt, later funding |
| 6. The long pause | 1953–54 | What cannot be frozen while a production waits? | Investigate season, performers, and financing installments |
| 7. The final print | 1955 | What is needed for a promised first screening? | Connect editing, soundtrack, print, and screening deadline |
| 8. The world sees it | 1955–56 | Does an important film become famous all at once? | Compare MoMA premiere, Indian theatrical release, Cannes prize |

### Documented narrative spine

**Vision → Rejection → Risk → Unexpected setback → Halt → Renewed support → Deadline → Reception.** Users never “play Ray” or rewrite historical choices: hypothetical exercises clarify filmmaking constraints and then reveal the documented events.

## Historical research anchors

- **Ray's 1970 BFI interview:** he discussed illustrating an edition of the novel, seeing *Bicycle Thieves* in 1950, unsuccessful pitching, financing through insurance/personal valuables, and roughly 4,000 feet of material before the production stopped. [BFI, 1970 interview republished 2021](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/features/satyajit-ray-conversation-1970-folke-isaksson)
- **Production account:** initial October 1952 train-field shooting, cattle grazing the kaash flowers, long pauses and eventual government assistance, score recording and finishing. [Satyajit Ray Org — Making of Pather Panchali](https://satyajitray.org/making-of-pather-panchali/)
- **Period financing and collaborators:** work began on weekends/holidays with a largely inexperienced group; West Bengal support permitted completion. [Ramon Magsaysay Award Foundation citation](https://rmaward.asia/rmawardees/ray-satyajit/)
- **The 1955 MoMA screening:** May world premiere and the museum's involvement. [MoMA curator account](https://www.moma.org/explore/inside_out/2012/11/13/satyajit-rays-pather-panchali/)
- **Date and official credits:** Indian release 26 August 1955; Subrata Mitra on photography, Bansi Chandragupta on art direction, Dulal Dutta editing, Ravi Shankar music. [Satyajit Ray Society](https://www.satyajitraysociety.org/cast_and_crew.html)
- **Film's themes and character treatment:** [Criterion Collection](https://www.criterion.com/films/28021-pather-panchali)
- **Early team's camera experience:** [ARRI timeline](https://100.arri.com/timeline/event/59ad909227fc605792b770a3)

## Fact and storytelling controls

- Do **not** invent Ray's thoughts, conversations, private emotions, production dialogue, actors' reactions, or financial amounts.
- Source-specific details must retain their provenance. The precise October shooting *day* is not asserted in the pilot because sources recount filming on Sundays alongside a numbered date not aligned to that weekday.
- The cattle incident is based on a retrospective first-person account; the continuity control is **an original schematic exercise**, never a claim to recreate the actual footage.
- Ray's 1970 interview is direct testimony, but it is not a contemporaneous production ledger.
- Do not represent original atmospheric artwork as archival photographs.
- Do not copy film clips, stills, Ray's voice, Ravi Shankar's music, or copyrighted illustrations without permission.
- Reception must distinguish **May 1955 MoMA premiere**, **August 1955 Indian theatrical release**, and **1956 Cannes recognition** rather than implying instant universal success.

## User experience rules

1. At each act, the viewer understands what changed and why the next scene matters.
2. An interaction can teach about the production only when it emerges from a documented constraint.
3. A source drawer identifies the historical basis for each act.
4. Skip and keyboard navigation remain possible; no unskippable title sequence or autoplay audio.
5. Original illustrations are clearly labeled.
6. Reflection notes ask what was learned, not whether animation looked pretty.

## Pilot acceptance criteria

Recruit 5–8 aspiring filmmakers/cinephiles. After using the pilot, ask users to recount the chronology and name the main production setbacks **without looking at the screen**. Check whether they can distinguish historical facts from simulations, want to continue beyond the chapter, and see any renewed purpose in the current Studio and Editing Theatre.

## Production boundary

This branch is for narrative and editorial validation only. **No edits to `www/`, `wrangler.jsonc`, Capacitor configuration, or Android workflows**. Public V6 remains a stable reference.
